# Math Lab
**Explore mathematics. Investigate questions. Learn together.**

Math Lab is a student-centered initiative built around a simple idea: mathematics is more than a subject to study — it is something to investigate, question, and discover together.

Through collaborative mathematical investigations and locally organized learning communities, Math Lab aims to give students opportunities to engage more deeply with mathematics, develop their reasoning skills, and participate in a wider community of mathematical exploration.

## Why Math Lab Exists

Mathematics education can become focused on memorizing formulas, following procedures, and arriving at correct answers. These skills have their place, but they are only part of what mathematics offers.

Mathematics is also about recognizing patterns, asking questions, testing conjectures, constructing proofs, exploring unfamiliar problems, and understanding *why* something works.

Math Lab exists to help students experience this side of mathematics.

We want to create opportunities for students to move beyond simply receiving information and toward actively investigating mathematical ideas. We also want these opportunities to extend beyond a single classroom, school, or community.

Our goal is to build a community in which mathematical curiosity can become meaningful, sustained participation.

## What We Do

### National Investigations

National Investigations are a central part of Math Lab's developing program.

They provide a shared mathematical investigation that students can explore through participating Local Labs. Rather than focusing exclusively on routine exercises, investigations are intended to encourage deeper reasoning, problem-solving, and engagement with mathematical questions.

Each investigation can serve as a common starting point for students in different communities to explore the same mathematical ideas.

### Local Labs

Local Labs are community-based groups that help students participate in Math Lab's activities.

A Local Lab can operate in a school, library, community center, or another suitable learning environment. Local facilitators help organize participation and connect students with Math Lab's investigations and resources.

This structure allows mathematical exploration to take place locally while remaining connected to a broader network of learners.

### Mathematical Learning Resources

Math Lab develops and coordinates learning resources to support its activities.

These may include investigation materials, problem sets, explanations, solutions, facilitator resources, and curricula for particular mathematical topics.

Resources can be developed centrally or in response to the needs of participating Local Labs, helping make mathematical activities more accessible and easier to organize.

### Research Into Mathematics Education

Math Lab also has the potential to investigate how students learn mathematics and which approaches best support their understanding.

Questions about mathematical misconceptions, alternative explanations, problem-solving strategies, and adaptive learning can become subjects of systematic investigation.

Where appropriate, these projects should use clear research questions, measurable outcomes, reproducible methods, and suitable ethical safeguards.

## Our Principles

**Curiosity before memorization.** We value asking questions, exploring ideas, and understanding mathematical reasoning rather than treating mathematics as a collection of formulas to remember.

**Reasoning matters.** An answer is important, but so are the arguments, methods, and insights that lead to it.

**Collaboration expands opportunity.** Students should have opportunities to learn with others, exchange approaches, discuss ideas, and contribute to shared investigations.

**Local communities, shared exploration.** Students should be able to participate in mathematical activities through their own schools and communities while connecting with a wider network.

**Accessibility and inclusion.** Mathematical exploration should welcome students with different backgrounds, experiences, and learning needs. We should continue exploring ways to make participation and understanding more accessible.

**Evidence over assumptions.** When Math Lab investigates educational approaches, conclusions should follow from the evidence rather than from expectations about what ought to work.

**Build, evaluate, improve.** Our programs and resources should develop through feedback, reflection, and careful evaluation.

## Who Can Participate?

Math Lab aims to bring together students who are curious about mathematics and people interested in helping them explore it.

Participation may include:

- Students interested in mathematical problem-solving and investigation.
- Students who want to explore mathematics beyond their usual coursework.
- Local facilitators who want to organize mathematical activities in their communities.
- Contributors who develop problems, curricula, explanations, and educational resources.
- Researchers and collaborators interested in mathematical learning and education.

You do not need to have all the answers to begin exploring mathematics. Curiosity, persistence, and a willingness to reason carefully are valuable starting points.

## How Math Lab Works

Math Lab is developing a structure that connects shared mathematical activities with local participation.

1. **Develop an investigation.** Define a mathematical question, prepare appropriate problems, and establish supporting resources.
2. **Connect participating Local Labs.** Make the investigation available to local facilitators and students.
3. **Explore the mathematics.** Participants work through the questions, discuss their reasoning, and investigate possible solutions.
4. **Reflect and improve.** Use feedback and appropriate evaluation to improve future investigations and resources.

The details of each activity may differ, but the underlying purpose remains the same: to create meaningful opportunities for students to engage with mathematics.

## Our Direction

Math Lab is building the foundations needed to make this vision practical: a network of Local Labs, well-designed National Investigations, useful educational resources, and a collaborative team.

As the initiative develops, we aim to strengthen both the quality of its mathematical activities and the opportunities available to students who participate.

We see mathematical investigation not simply as a way to practice mathematics, but as a way to experience how mathematical thinking works.
